## Intention and style
A confident, premium, fast introduction of a brand or person (a company promo, an agency intro, a personal-brand intro), spoken by one presenter on several locations. It feels like an ad, not a vlog: few cuts while he talks, but the picture never sits still (zooms in and out of long takes), big white typographic lockups land on the key phrases (often behind his head), the brand's logo appears when its name is said, and in the middle the voice stops for a fast montage cut on the music's beat. It ends cleanly on a CTA and the logo on black. Aggressive = fast snaps, hard rhythm, sound hits on the transitions; impressive = depth (text behind the head), scale (reveals), proof (montage, famous logos).

## Relationships and dependencies
- Captions, lockups and graphics are inside the zoom layer: they scale with `zoom-push-hold-release`.
- While a lockup word is spoken, the chest caption is hidden for that word; the brand logo also replaces the caption.
- Lockups, logo stacks and the CTA sit behind the speaker when they overlap him (subject matte needed); the face is never covered.
- The montage always starts out of the number title moment (its pixel breakup crosses the first montage cut) and ends with the white flash on a beat.
- Echo + spin only once, on the strongest music moment.
- Never stack two sounds on one moment (a whip gets only the whoosh, not also a click).

## Triggers and sequencing principles
- Opening (0–~5 s): frame 1 starts tight and snaps out (`zoom-open-fast-pullout-blur`) while the addressee lockup builds behind the head; a cut to a closer take every ~1.4 s; the arc arrow for a time statement; the glow flicker hides a take change; a stepped push-in lands on the two promise words with a lockup ("NEXT / LEVEL") and holds to the cut.
- Body: each new location opens with a cut (the first one with `zoom-cut-in-tight-pull-reveal` + a lockup in the revealed space). Inside long takes (5–20 s) one push-hold-release every ~3–10 s on key phrases. Brand name → logo. Famous examples → logo stack, inside a push-in.
- Peak: the claim ("number one") → dip to black → big number → pixel breakup → voice-free beat montage (~8–14 s) → voice returns over the last shot → white flash back to the speaker, landing tight and releasing.
- Ending: a walk toward camera, the CTA lockup behind the head + logo, the footage fades to black, end card ~1.3 s, music fades out.

## Rhythm and emphasis
- Talking sections: the rhythm comes from zooms and lockups, not cuts. Hook cuts ~every 1.4 s, later takes 5–20 s. Lockups ≈ every 5–15 s; push-ins never closer than ~1 s apart; never a lockup and a push-in on every sentence.
- Montage: one shot per beat (~0.65 s at ~90 BPM), cuts on the kick, whips on about half of the cuts.
- Each section gets its own location.

## Audio-visual coordination
**Music (measured from the reference):**
- Bass/music enters on the first cut of the hook (≈ 1.4 s).
- Under speech: a quiet bed, ≈ 20–28 dB under the speech peaks.
- Montage: music alone at full level (≈ speech loudness); every montage cut on a kick/bass hit; the echo + spin on the drop; the white flash on a beat.
- End: music fades out over the black end card (~1.1 s).
- The user chooses the track (standing rule). The track needs a clear beat and a drop for the montage.

## Scaling to other lengths and structures
- Shorter (~30 s): snap-out hook + one lockup, one push-hold-release, a 6–8 s montage, CTA card.
- Longer (60–90 s): more locations; keep one montage; add a second logo/brand moment rather than more lockups.
- No B-roll: drop the number title and the montage (and their sounds); keep zooms, lockups, logo moments and the CTA.
- Seated/indoor footage: lockups above the head; push-ins smaller (~1.1–1.2x).
- Without a famous-names list or a time statement: skip the logo stack / arc arrow; never invent content to use them.

## Template-specific adaptations and constraints
- Lockups, captions and the CTA follow the selected brand's typeface and colours (white by default); the brand logo is the selected brand's.
- The speaker's footage keeps the project grade (user rule: warm, bright, never grey or dark).
- Keep everything important out of the red zones (B6).
- Third-party logos (logo stack) only when the user confirms they may be shown.

## Success criteria
- The first second hits: frame 1 tight, snap out, lockup building.
- During speech the picture is never static for long, yet cuts stay rare.
- Every key phrase gets a lockup or a push-in, not every sentence.
- The montage is clearly on the beat and returns with a white flash on a beat.
- Every planned sound lands within ~1 frame of its visual event and never covers a word.
- The last second shows only the CTA and the brand logo.
