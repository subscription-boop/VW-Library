# sfx-whoosh-main-start

> Library technique (category: `sfx`). Shared rules: [CONTRACT](../../../library/CONTRACT.md). To apply this to a video, follow [QUICK-CREATE](../../../library/QUICK-CREATE.md) (full rules: [EXECUTION](../../../library/EXECUTION.md)). The name is an identifier only: read this document for behavior.
> Execution part only. Analysis notes (reference measurements): [ANALYSIS.md](ANALYSIS.md) - open only when a decision needs them.

## Observable behavior
At the moment the video moves from its hook/set-up into the main content (here: the white "3 steps." card cutting to "Step 1"), a quiet airy whoosh swells and fades over ~0.15–0.3 s and ends right on the cut. It is bright and breathy, with little or no low end, sitting under the voice.

## Intended effect
Signals "here we go": the main content starts, the viewer's attention resets.

## Defining characteristics
- One sound, once per video, at the entry into the main content. [User]
- Ends on (or within a frame or two of) the cut. [Rule]
- Soft and short: airy whoosh or swish, no heavy impact, no long riser. [Rule]
- Quiet: clearly under the voice. [Rule]

## When to use
- Triggers: the cut from the hook/intro/set-up into the first step, tip or main point.
- Avoid when: the user asked for no sound effects; the transition already has a different sound.

## Relationships
- Sits on the cut out of `card-white-word-beat` (or the hook) into the first `text-step-label-serif` section.
- Does not replace other sound design; do not add whooshes to every cut.

## Adaptation
- Sound source: the user's own SFX library (for example a short whoosh from the user's SFX pack); ask before generating one. Trim so the tail ends on the cut.
- Level: roughly 15–20 dB under the voice peak; adjust by ear/measurement on the final mix.

## Key numbers
- Reference burst before the cut to "Step 1": ~0.15 s, energy centred ~6–7 kHz, almost nothing below 1 kHz, smooth swell and fade (observed; table in ANALYSIS.md).
- Origin of that reference sound is uncertain: an added whoosh, or a breath/sibilant left in the speaker's audio (not listened to). No similar sound was found at the other section cuts.

## Success criteria
- The viewer feels a light lift into the main content; nobody notices it as an "effect".
