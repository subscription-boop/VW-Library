# title-rank-number-pixel-breakup

> Library technique (category: `title`). Shared rules: [CONTRACT](../../../library/CONTRACT.md). To apply this to a video, follow [QUICK-CREATE](../../../library/QUICK-CREATE.md) (full rules: [EXECUTION](../../../library/EXECUTION.md)). The name is an identifier only: read this document for behavior.
> Execution part only. Analysis notes (reference measurements, overlaps, history): [ANALYSIS.md](ANALYSIS.md) - open only when a decision needs them.

## Observable behavior
1. "…so you could be **number one**": on "be", a small heavy white "NO." fades in from grey next to the speaker's head (upper left).
2. The talking shot **dips to black** in ~6 frames, ~2 frames of black.
3. **Fade up** (~0.4 s) on a moody skyline B-roll at dusk. A **giant heavy "1"** (≈ 0.4 W wide, ≈ 0.3 H tall) stands in the middle third over the skyline, with the small "NO." at its upper left. The number fades up with the picture. The scene slowly pulls out (~0.85x over ~0.5 s).
4. **Pixel breakup:** starting with "NO.", then the "1", the letters break into square pixel blocks of mixed sizes that scatter and flicker away (~8 frames). The last blocks stay over the first frames of the next shot (the montage) for ~4–6 frames after the cut, then vanish.
The voice stops here; the music takes over (see `cut-beat-broll-montage` ([SKILL.md](../cut-beat-broll-montage/SKILL.md))).

## Intended effect
Turns a claim into a cinematic chapter break and a launch pad for the montage; the breakup feels digital and energetic and hides the cut.

## Defining characteristics
- Claim word appears small beside the speaker first, then the big number on its own scene. [Rule]
- Quick dip to black (≈ 0.2 s out, ≈ 0.1 s black, ≈ 0.4 s up). [Rule]
- Giant heavy numeral over a B-roll scene in the middle third. [Fixed]
- Exit by pixel-block disintegration that continues over the cut. [Fixed]

## When to use
- Triggers: "number one", "#1", "top 3", "100 %" — a rank or big number claim that leads into a showcase.
- Avoid when: the claim is a passing remark, or no strong B-roll scene is available.

## Anatomy over time
- Start: "NO." on the word before the number (~0.3 s before the dip).
- Development: dip → fade up with the number (~0.6 s total), hold/pull-out ~0.4 s.
- Resolution: pixel breakup ~0.25 s, crossing the cut by ~0.15 s.
- Repetition: once.

## Relationships
- Speech: the number scene can run without speech; voice resumes later.
- Music: the dip and the cut out of the scene sit on music hits (≈ 00:16.35 and 00:16.95 in the reference).
- Sound effects: none observed beyond the music.

## Adaptation
- Adaptable: the number/claim text; the B-roll scene (city, product, team).
- Brand: numeral in the brand heavy sans, white (or brand accent).

## Key numbers
- "NO." fades in beside the head ~3 frames grey → white (00:15.72) (observed; table in ANALYSIS.md).
- Dip to black ~5 frames (00:16.02–00:16.18) (observed; table in ANALYSIS.md).
- Pixel breakup of "NO." then "1": blocks grow and scatter (00:16.55–00:16.95) (observed; table in ANALYSIS.md).
- Leftover blocks over the next shot ~6 frames (00:16.95–00:17.15) (observed; table in ANALYSIS.md).

These describe the references. They are not quotas or required values.

## Success criteria
- The claim reads as a title moment; the number is huge and clean before it breaks.
- The breakup visibly crosses the cut and feels digital, not like a plain fade.
