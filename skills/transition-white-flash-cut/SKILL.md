# transition-white-flash-cut

> Library technique (category: `transition`). Shared rules: [CONTRACT](../../../library/CONTRACT.md). To apply this to a video, follow [QUICK-CREATE](../../../library/QUICK-CREATE.md) (full rules: [EXECUTION](../../../library/EXECUTION.md)). The name is an identifier only: read this document for behavior.
> Execution part only. Analysis notes (reference measurements, overlaps, history): [ANALYSIS.md](ANALYSIS.md) - open only when a decision needs them.

## Observable behavior
At the end of the B-roll montage, the last shot brightens sharply; the cut lands on an almost fully white frame where only a faint ghost of the new shot (the speaker on the dock) is visible; over the next ~4 frames the new shot comes back from the white to normal exposure. Clean neutral white, no colour tint, no streaks. In the reference the new shot also starts zoomed in and then releases out (`zoom-push-hold-release` ([SKILL.md](../zoom-push-hold-release/SKILL.md))).

## Intended effect
A bright "reset" that marks the return from the montage to the speaker with energy.

## Defining characteristics
- Neutral white bloom, peak at the cut. [Fixed]
- Short: ~1–2 frames up, ~4 frames down. [Rule]
- Single pulse (no flicker). [Rule]

## When to use
- Triggers: return from a montage/B-roll burst to the talking head; a strong section change on a music hit.
- Avoid when: the next shot opens on a quiet emotional line.

## Anatomy over time
- Start: 1–2 frames before the cut.
- Development: peak white on the first frame of the new shot.
- Resolution: exposure recovers over ~4 frames.
- Repetition: once.

## Relationships
- Music: lands on a bass hit (≈ 00:29.49 low-frequency onset).
- Speech: the speaker's line ("Coca-Cola,") starts as the white clears.
- Sound effects: none observed beyond the music.

## Adaptation
- Brand: keep neutral white.

## Key numbers
- Cut + near-white frames ~2–3 frames; recovery to normal ~4 frames (observed; table in ANALYSIS.md).

## Success criteria
- One clean white pulse that hides the cut; the speaker is readable again within ~0.15 s.
